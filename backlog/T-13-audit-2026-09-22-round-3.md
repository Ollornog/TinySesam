# T-13 — third audit, 2026-09-22

After [T-9](T-9-audit-2026-09-21-round-2.md), a third pass, this time as **red team against
blue team**: twelve online research tracks (sessions, CSRF, OIDC, WebAuthn, TOTP, reset/magic link,
forward auth, LDAP/SAML, rate limits, stack CVEs, admin/API keys, supply chain) supplied the
attack lists; eight attackers had to back every finding with a working proof of concept,
six defenders checked against ASVS 5.0, NIST SP 800-63B, configuration baselines, supply chain,
logging and failure behaviour. Every finding then went through an independent reviewer, who
reproduced it and tried to refute it.

**155 raw findings → 139 confirmed, 6 refuted or already resolved, 4 duplicates, 0 unclear.**
Severity after review: **15 high, 44 medium, 69 low, 11 notes.**
Some of these (F-13, discovery issuer) are already fixed (#55).

What sets this round apart from the two before it: the protocol checks hold — 88 targeted
counter-probes came to nothing (session fixation, `alg=none`, HS256 confusion, open redirect,
flow binding). **The holes are one layer deeper:** in linking verified
foreign identities to local accounts, in the trust anchors of the configuration (`base_url`,
Host header, discovery), in machine credentials (an API key inherits `is_admin` despite its scope) and
in self-service without fresh authentication. **17 of the confirmed findings are
incompletely closed commitments from T-8, T-9, T-10 or 0.18.0** — a ticked-off item is
currently not reliable evidence in this project; every fix here needs a test that covers the
class, not just the single case.

As in T-8/T-9, every item is first reproduced against the code before it is touched.

## What blocks 1.0 (16 items)

Six architecture questions: **F-11** (SAML/LDAP bind via the username), **F-12**
(LDAP transport), **F-16** (SP identity taken from the Host header), **R6-5** (API-key scope does not touch
`is_admin`), **R3-1** (self-enrollment versus `login_chain`), **B2-10** (passkey without
user verification as the sole factor). Ten one- to five-liners: **F-14**, **F-28**, **B5-03**,
**R4-01**, **R4-12**, **R4-10**, **B2-1**, **R3-3**, **B4-1** — and **F-13**, already done.
Everything else is hardening with a plan, not a blocker.

**Three chains are heavier than their links:** R6-5 + R3-3 + R6-6 + R6-4 = instance takeover
from a leaked CI key · R4-12 + R4-10 = a quiet, unlimited password oracle against any account
· B5-03 + R6-1 = first-admin token in the world-readable `security.log`. **Four root fixes close
half a dozen findings each:** make `base_url` mandatory for federation/mail; count and record failed attempts
atomically; one response middleware for security headers; a
`stepup_fresh()` gate in front of the five self-service routes.

## Second attack round against the fixes of this round (2026-09-22)

The line "Done when … a fourth pass attacks the fixes themselves" is redeemed: the
fixes from T-13 were tested against themselves and reworked in four groups — **M1**
(docs and unmeasured commitments), **M2** (one rule for the public base), **M3**
(`email_verified`, one semantics across all login paths), **M4** (counters and locks). The
individual findings are listed at the item whose fix they attacked (F-14, R4-01, R4-12,
R4-10, R3-3) and as an item of their own where they had no predecessor (B-bypass-8 at
`set_email`). The experience from T-9 was confirmed: **The regressions lay in the fixes,
not in the legacy code** — and half of the findings were commitments that nobody measured.

  assigned identifier that belongs to a different account (atomic, cross-wise, also `Alice`/`alice`);
  race `IntegrityError` → functional answer (409/neutral/400), mail-mode switch in one
  transaction, startup message complete. Test: `tests/test_identifier_space.py`, 21 mutations red.
- **G2 — limit a complete: joining instead of creation.** One id waterline per identifier
  (`users.name_versuch_ab`/`mail_versuch_ab`/`name_audit_ab`/`mail_audit_ab`), set on creation,
  rename, address change and addendum in the same UPDATE (`CASE`: same bucket → stays), on
  a foreign writer by the recalculation trigger to the current state. Readers: `sperre_aufheben`,
  `record_login` (`_cleanup_limit`), deleting, `own_events`; existing data (NULL) → creation as before. The
  series is deliberately cleared completely (rationale in `sperre_aufheben`). Test:
  `tests/test_identifier_join.py`, 14 mutations red.
- **G5 — LDAP rename.** `federated_identity.name_topf`: the typed directory name, if it is
  not the name/address of the account. `Store.count_identifiers` (with guard: not if it belongs to a
  different account) for full login, panel reset, `passwd`, `unlock`; `unlock` takes the
  identifier as typed and clears even without an account (existing data). Test: `tests/test_t13_decisions.py`
  (B2-6 × directory), 7 mutations red.
- **G5-N1 — directory login clears foreign counters.** A filter via `mail` resolves the address
  of a local account to a third party; their login cleared the account window of the
  owner. `record_login(…, konto=…)` with the same guard (`Store.bucket_of_another`). Test:
  ibid., 2 mutations red.
- **G7 — PIN behind another factor (R2-2 limit).** The kind of the series now follows the path:
  half session (chain step, `pending_user` like `/auth/totp`) or full (route chain) → kind
  `pin_folge`, which the self-service reset leaves standing like TOTP; without session → `pin` as before.
  `begin_attempt(series_kind=…)`, the pre-booking remembers the booked kind. Test:
  `tests/test_pin_sequence.py`, 21 mutations red.
- **G7-N1 — dead end `pin_login=False` in a chain.** The PIN step after the password ran via
  the guest path (404). `/auth/pin` recognises the half session (GET without name field, POST checks its
  PIN); `pin_login` applies only to the guest path.
- **G7-N2 — guest PIN oracle in the strict chain.** `TinySesamConfig.pin_as_first_factor()`: in a
  strict chain with the PIN behind another factor it is no first factor (login page without field,
  `/auth/pin` without session 404). Not strict, it remains one — the configuration check warns.
- **G9 — first window of an LDAP outage.** The pre-booking of the login route is pending with LDAP
  (`login_attempt.offen`); whoever would fail only because of pending pre-bookings waits (at most
  `CONNECTION_TIMEOUT + 2` s, 8 slots per process), otherwise 429 with `deferred login` — no lock,
  no fail2ban hit, no lock mail. Open rows older than 30 s count as a failed attempt; R7-2 stays
  (the salvo waits along). Test: `tests/test_prebooking_pending.py`, 22 mutations red.
- **G1 — binding via the name without limit (case 4).** Deadline `federation_name_binding_days`

### Attack round 2026-09-26 (on the limit fixes)

Occasion: seven commits, a dozen places in identity, binding and counters. Two independent
attackers with PoC, one focus each.

**a1 — identity and rights: two proven findings, both fixed.**

- **a1-01 (high) — a name from another source bypassed G2-N.** The marker
  `name_selbst_gewaehlt` arose only on registration and renaming; OIDC, SAML and
  LDAP creation carried 0. Measured: `chefin` via OIDC (`preferred_username` chosen by themselves at the IdP),
  then the real chefin via LDAP — case 4 bound her identifier to the OIDC account, the role from
  `ldap_group_role_map` came along, the attacker kept signing in via OIDC; via SAML likewise up to
  the admin flag. Conversely a SAML self-registrant with `uid=chefin` took over an account created by LDAP
  directly. **Built:** column `users.name_quelle` (additive, schema stays 11),
  set on creation by the source (`_create_account`, not public), deleted on
  renaming; `_rebinding_reason` rejects every OTHER source (`name_aus_quelle`), the operator
  opens it with `loese_fremde_bindung`. Existing data backfilled once (`NAME_SOURCE_BACKFILLED`): OIDC
  from `oidc_identity`, LDAP/SAML from a binding from the second of creation (±2 s). **Deviation
  from the recommendation** ("marker self-chosen for every federated creation"): It would break the replacement
  of the own placeholder — an LDAP account without identifier would be rejected per `ldap_attr_id`; the
  counter-probe turns `tests/test_ldap.py` (A-4) red. Test: `tests/test_federation_legacy.py`
  (PoC of both variants, opposite direction, placeholder, renaming, existing binding, backfill),
  13 mutations red, plus the counter-probe of the recommendation (2× red).
- **a1-02 (low–medium) — "address taken?" via the account page of an LDAP user.** G12a
  counted the dispatch in the database, the rejection of a taken address only in memory: per
  worker and restart one more visible row (measured `[1,1,1,1,1]` free versus `[1,2,2,2,3]`
  taken). **Built:** The rows `email_change_*` from this path begin with
  `konto=<id> quelle=<quelle>` (`_request_change`, the same for every result), and
  `Store.source_mail_since` counts dispatch AND rejection (`SOURCE_MAIL_DONE`); deadline
  `max(1 Tag, email_change_ttl_min)` — otherwise a long-lived open link would hold up the free address
  longer than the rejection holds up the taken one. The memory now only dampens transient things. Test:
  `tests/test_source_address.py` (a1-02: free/taken/reserved across two workers and a
  restart, next day, link validity 3 days), 6 mutations red. **Remains:** If the
  dispatch to a free address fails, the next login tries again after the mail window —
  a taken one has no dispatch that could fail. Triggerable only via a mail server
  error. **Deliberately so, with reason:** No user controls the mail server's error; a never
  delivered link must not hold up the next one for a day (G12a). The same has applied since
  2026-09-27 to a full queue (review round, part 2).

**a1, suspected cases checked, deliberately not built:**

- *Locking out via a same-named local account (G5).* Whoever creates locally an account with exactly the
  directory name of a person removes that bucket from `count_identifiers` (intended: no
  clearing of foreign counters); no self-service path then clears the person's series under that name.
  This is the general lock DoS of B2-6 (anyone can make 100 failed attempts under any name)
  plus name squatting, not a permanent lock: `tinysesam unlock <name>` always clears under the typed
  identifier. Prevention is operational: do not mix registration and LDAP. Since 2026-09-27 (review round,
  part 1, A) a recorded directory name is taken for every other account; only an unrecorded one
  can still be claimed (renamed in the directory, no login since). LDAP login
  then rejects (C), the remedy is renaming the local account. **Deliberately so, with reason.**
- *The existing-data backfill of G2-N also marks renamings by the operator.* Direction safe
  (only rejects), and concerns only databases from the unpublished state — 0.20.1 knew no
  renaming, so there is no `username_changed` row there. A match on `durch=betreiber` in the
  detail would be forgeable via a name with spaces.

**Tight (a1):** G6 order in all three paths, order in `_rebinding_reason`
(identifier conflict before the opened binding), `maybe_promote_admin` (address/name separate, SAML/LDAP without
proof fail-closed), G12c triggers including the functional answers, the self-service address change
(G12b), `change_username`, G10.

**a2 — locks and counters: aborted, without finding and without check** (→ made up as review round
locks/counters 2026-09-27, below). The run ended at a security block of the tool before
a PoC arose. Thus unchecked by an attacker remain id waterlines (G2), series and
clearing paths (`pin_folge`, reset, `_end_series`, `unlock`),
`count_identifiers`/`record_login(konto=…)` (G5, G5-N1), pending pre-bookings (G9), PIN chain step
and guest path (G7), source mail counting, completeness of the anonymisation (G4). They are evidenced by
the regression tests and mutations of their commits (G2 14, G5/G5-N1 9, G7 21, G9 22, G4 6, G12a/b
20), not by an attack.
